About

How I work

Four things I keep coming back to.

01
Own it end to end.

I take responsibility for the full arc — discovery, design, implementation, measurement. The handoff is where most product work goes to die. I work to remove it. The Support Portal is the most direct version of this: I shipped a designer-led production build, then partnered with engineering to take it the rest of the way.

02
The work that matters most is rarely on the brief.

The decisions that compound — architectural choices, system flexibility, infrastructure for the next project — are usually invisible to whoever wrote the brief. I make them anyway. The iCruise white-label architecture wasn't asked for; it produced three different business wins across a year.

03
In a pitch defense, the design has to do the convincing.

When the stakes are real and the timeline is short, the question isn't what's the best design — it's what's the artifact that wins this meeting? Sometimes those answers are the same. Often they're not. The NEA Hub was a one-week prototype; it was the artifact that kept a client from leaving.

04
Redesign your delivery model, not just the product.

The biggest thing AI is doing for design isn't generating moodboards — it's collapsing the design-to-production handoff. I'm increasingly interested in the systems that let one designer ship what used to take a team. That's the through-line on the work I want to do next.
